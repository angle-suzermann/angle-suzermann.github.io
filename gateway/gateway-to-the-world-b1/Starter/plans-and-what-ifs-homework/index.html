Homework: Plans and What-ifs

About 25–30 minutes. Your answers are saved on this device, so you can stop and come back.

Remember: am / is / are + -ing for arranged plans · If + past, would + verb for imaginary situations · tag questions use the same helper verb · good at + -ing

Exercise 1. Arrangement or decision?

Click the correct form. Arranged plans (with a time, a person, tickets) use am / is / are + -ing. Quick decisions and predictions use will.

1. Mum and I ___ the dentist at four o’clock tomorrow. It’s in my diary.

2. I think it ___ tomorrow.

3. “The phone is ringing!” — “OK, I ___ it.”

4. We ___ to Kazan on Saturday. We have bought the tickets.

5. “I can’t carry these bags.” — “Don’t worry, I ___ you.”

Exercise 2. My diary for next week

Write the verb in the Present Continuous. Short forms are OK.

Monday: I (visit) my grandma.

Tuesday: Dad (take) me to the swimming pool.

Wednesday: We (not have) lessons because it’s a holiday.

Thursday: (you / come) to Sasha’s party?

Friday: Mum and her friend (see) a film.

Rule 1. Second conditional: imaginary situations

If + Past Simple, would + verb → If I found €100, I would give it to the police.

The verb after if looks like the past, but the meaning is now or the future: we are imagining something unlikely.

Question: What would you do if you saw a lion in the street?

With be we can say were for all persons: If I were you, I’d wait.

If I will have a dog, I would walk it. → If I had a dog, I would walk it.

Exercise 4. Write the verbs

Use the Second Conditional. In the first sentence you can write was or were.

3. She (be) happier if she (have) a dog.

6. My friend (help) me if I (ask) him.

Rule 2. Tag questions

Positive sentence → negative tag: You like pizza, don’t you?

Negative sentence → positive tag: They aren’t coming, are they?

Use the same helper verb as in the sentence (is, can, have…). No helper verb? Present Simple → do / does, Past Simple → did.

You won a prize, didn’t you? · Tom can swim, can’t he? · It’s cold today, isn’t it?

Rule 3. Verb + -ing

After a preposition (good at, interested in, look forward to) and after enjoy, keep, finish → verb + -ing.

She is good at drawing. · I enjoy playing with my friends. · I’m looking forward to seeing you.

try + -ing = test something to see if it helps: Try restarting the computer.   try to + verb = make an effort: I’m trying to learn Chopin’s waltz.
