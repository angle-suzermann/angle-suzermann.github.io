Rule 1. used to = a habit or a state in the past that is not true now

I used to live in a village. (I don’t live there now.)

Negative: didn’t use to + verb → We didn’t use to have a TV.

Question: Did + subject + use to + verb? → Did you use to play chess?

didn’t used to → didn’t use to   (after did / didn’t the verb is in its basic form: use, not used)

Rule 2. Not for one-time events

I used to go to Spain in 2019. → I went to Spain in 2019.

used to is for things that happened again and again, or lasted a long time. A single event with a date or yesterday / last year is the Past Simple.

Reading: Grandma Vera’s childhood

My grandma Vera grew up in a small village in the 1960s. Life there was very different from mine. She didn’t use to have a phone or a TV, so in the evenings the family used to sit together and listen to the radio. Every morning she walked three kilometres to school, even in winter. “My feet were always cold,” she says with a smile. At weekends she used to help her mother bake bread, and she still makes it today.

Vera has been living in the city for thirty years now, but she misses the village. She has been teaching me to bake for two months, and I’m getting better. Last Sunday my bread was almost as good as hers — almost!

Rule 3. have / has been + -ing

We use it for an action that began in the past and is still going on now, or that has just stopped and we can see the result.

I have been learning English for three years. · She has been waiting since four o’clock.

Negative: haven’t / hasn’t been + -ing. Question: How long have you been living here?

You look tired. — Yes, I’ve been studying all day.

She is waiting since eight. → She has been waiting since eight.   I’d been waiting here for an hour (said now) → I’ve been waiting here for an hour.

Rule 4. for or since?

for + a period of time (how long): for two hours, for ten years

since + the moment it started: since Monday, since 2020, since I was born

How to do “Find and correct”

1. Find the verb. 2. Ask: past habit? still going on? one-time event? 3. Write only the correct verb form.

Example: She is waiting since eight. → has been waiting. One sentence is correct — write OK. Every other sentence has a mistake!

Speaking: ask and answer

Ask your partner or your teacher. Use used to and have been + -ing. Write short notes (not checked).

  1. What did you use to do when you were five that you don’t do now?
  2. Did you use to have a favourite toy or cartoon? Tell me about it.
  3. How long have you been learning English? What have you been doing this week to improve it?
  4. What has your family been doing this month?
  5. Ask your teacher: What did you use to do on Saturdays when you were my age?
