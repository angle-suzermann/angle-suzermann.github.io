Maths in English · Lesson 5 lexical version

Averages, spread, charts and basic probability. This topic is in the IB “prior learning” list even though it is not in your Russian geometry list. After this lesson you do the mock test.

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • use the English terms for averages, spread and charts (the mean, the median, the interquartile range);
  • read probability questions in English (at random, without replacement, mutually exclusive);
  • calculate averages, simple probabilities and expected values in English;
  • write a statistics or probability solution an examiner can follow.
Remember: среднее арифметическое is the mean; наугад is at random; 3 из 8 is 3 out of 8; игральный кубик is a dice, never a cube.

1. Read the question

Choose an answer. You see the explanation straight away.

1. среднее арифметическое in IB papers is…

IB says the mean. Average is everyday English and can mean the mean, median or mode.

2. “Find the median.” First you must…

The median is the middle value of the ordered data.

3. How do you read P(A′)?

A′ is “A prime”, the complement of A: P(A′) = 1 − P(A).

4. “Two balls are taken without replacement” means…

Without replacement: the second probability changes.

5. “A fair dice” means…

fair = правильный, симметричный.

6. “Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form.” 6/8 becomes…

Simplest form = cancel common factors: 6/8 = 3/4.

Question 1 gives the marks of a sample of 20 students. The total is 1 240, so the mean is 1 240 ÷ 20 = 62. Exactly 5 out of 20 students scored at least 70 marks, which is 25%.

In Question 2 one student is chosen at random, so all 20 students are equally likely to be chosen. The probability of choosing a student who scored at least 70 is 5/20 = 1/4.

Question 3: you roll a fair dice 60 times. The expected number of sixes is 60 × 1/6 = 10. In Question 4 you throw a fair coin twice, so P(two heads) = 1/4.

Question 5: the events A and B are mutually exclusive, so P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). Finally, in Question 6 you take two balls from a bag without replacement, so the probabilities change for the second ball.

A. How a solution is built

Problem 1. The mean of five numbers is 6. Four of them are 3, 5, 8 and 9. Find the fifth number.

These are the lines of a model solution, mixed up. Click them in the right order.

Let the fifth number be x.
The sum of the five numbers is 5 × 6 = 30.
So 3 + 5 + 8 + 9 + x = 30.
Therefore 25 + x = 30, which gives x = 5.

C. Fill in the solution phrases

Problem: A bag has 4 red and 6 blue balls. Two balls are taken at random without replacement. Find the probability that both are red. Complete the phrases. The first letters help you. Type the whole phrase.

taking a red ball first is 4/10. the first ball is not put back, the second ball is red is 3/9. P(both red) = 4/10 × 3/9 = 2/15.
