LIA Maths Placement Test · Lesson 1 lexical version

The London International Academy (Canada) Mathematics Placement Test lasts 2 hours and has four parts. You may use a non-graphing calculator. You already know most of the methods; this lesson fills the gaps (sequences, compound growth, quadratics) and teaches the Canadian words the questions use.

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • understand Canadian question words (slope, exponent, evaluate exactly, to the nearest tenth);
  • find terms and sums of arithmetic and geometric sequences;
  • solve compound-growth and decay problems;
  • write a quadratic from its zeros, read its vertex and describe a transformation.
Remember: Canadian maths says slope where IB says gradient, and exponent where IB says index or power. Always check units and the rounding instruction. They cost marks.

1. Read the question

Choose an answer. You see the explanation straight away.

1. “Evaluate exactly √50.” You should write…

Exactly means no decimals: √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2.

2. “Determine the slope of the line through (0, 1) and (2, 7).” Slope is…

Slope = gradient = rise ÷ run = 6 ÷ 2 = 3.

3. “Round to the nearest tenth.” 4.267 becomes…

Tenth = first decimal place. 4.267 → 4.3. (Hundredth would be 4.27.)

4. $1000 is invested at 3% compounded annually. This means that…

Each year the amount is multiplied by 1.03. After n years: 1000 × 1.03ⁿ.

5. “Simplify 3x + 2x − x.” The answer is…

Simplify = write in a shorter form. There is no “=” to solve. 3x + 2x − x = 4x.

6. In scientific notation 52 000 is…

In scientific notation the first number is between 1 and 10: 5.2 × 10⁴.

A. How a solution is built

Problem 1. The 4th term of an arithmetic sequence is 19 and the 9th term is 44. Determine the 20th term.

These are the lines of a model solution, mixed up. Click them in the right order.

Let a be the first term and d the common difference.
Then t₄ = a + 3d = 19 and t₉ = a + 8d = 44.
Subtracting the equations gives 5d = 25, so d = 5.
Substituting d = 5 into the first equation gives a = 19 − 15 = 4.
Therefore t₂₀ = 4 + 19 × 5 = 99.

4. Quadratics in Canadian English

Four short questions about graphs, then one about the equation. Choose an answer or type it.

How to type: × as x or *, powers with ^ (x² is x^2), π as pi, √ as sqrt, minus as -, fractions as 1/2. Spaces don’t matter.

1. y = (x − 3)² + 2 is the graph of y = x² translated…

(x − 3) moves the graph right. The vertex is (3, 2).

2. y = 2x² is…

Factor 2 makes the graph narrower: stretch. Factor ½ would be a compression.

3. y = −x² is…

Minus turns the graph upside down: reflection in the x-axis.

4. The vertex of y = (x + 1)² − 4 is…

x + 1 = 0 → x = −1. The vertex is (−1, −4).

5. A quadratic has zeros 2 and −3 and passes through (0, −12). Write it as f(x) = a(x − 2)(x + 3). Determine a.

Hint: put x = 0 into f(x) = a(x − 2)(x + 3) and solve for a.
