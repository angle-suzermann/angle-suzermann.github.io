Maths in English · Lesson 1 lexical version

Number and algebra: you already know this maths in Russian. Today you learn how it is written and asked in English, the way the IB entrance test will ask it.

By the end of this lesson you can:
  • use the English term for 30 core ideas in number and algebra;
  • see from the command word (find, show that, hence…) exactly what a question wants;
  • round and write numbers the English way (decimal point, significant figures, standard form);
  • write a solution in English that an examiner can follow and give method marks for.
Why this matters: in IB maths you get marks for the method, not only the final number. A correct answer with no working can lose most of the marks; clear working in English protects them.

1. Russian habits vs English conventions

Choose an answer. You see the explanation straight away.

1. In English, two and a half is written:

English uses a decimal point. A comma is never a decimal separator in IB papers.

2. How is 12000 written in IB papers?

IB separates thousands with a space, so nobody confuses a comma or a point with a decimal.

3. Russian tg 30° is written in English as:

Always tan. Likewise cot for ctg.

4. Unless the question says otherwise, IB wants answers that are not exact given…

The default rule: give exact answers, or answers correct to 3 significant figures.

5. 0.0456789 correct to 3 significant figures is:

Start counting at the first non-zero digit: 4, 5, 6 | 7 → round up → 0.0457.

6. What does “give the exact value of √8” want?

“Exact” means no rounding: keep surds, π and fractions.

7. How do you read x⁵ aloud?

x² = “x squared”, x³ = “x cubed”, x⁵ = “x to the power of five” (or “x to the fifth”). “In the fifth degree” is a Russian calque.

8. Which is the English way to write a mixed number?

Written as 2⅓ or 2 1/3, read “two and a third”. In algebra, improper fractions like 7/3 are usually clearer.

Question 1 asks you to solve the equation 3(x − 4) = 2x + 5. First expand the brackets: 3x − 12 = 2x + 5, so x is equal to 17. To check, substitute x = 17 into both sides: the left side is 3 × 13 = 39 and the right side is 2 × 17 + 5 = 39. Both sides are equal, so the answer is right.

In Question 2 a bike costs €400 and its price is increased by 15%. To find the new price, multiply 400 by 1.15, which gives €460. Not every price goes up by a round number, so use a calculator.

Question 3 asks you to write 0.000382 in the form a × 10ᵏ: the answer is 3.82 × 10⁻⁴. Question 4 wants 2 ÷ 7 correct to 3 significant figures. Your calculator shows 0.285714…, so the correct answer is 0.286.

In Question 5, 4x² = 36. First divide both sides by 4 to get x² = 9. Then take the square root of both sides: x = 3 or x = −3. Remember that 3 to the power of 2 and −3 to the power of 2 are both 9, so the equation has two roots.

Question 6: share 56 sweets in the ratio 3 : 5. There are 8 parts, and 56 ÷ 8 = 7, so the shares are 21 and 35. Finally, Question 7 asks if 1 236 is a multiple of 4. It is: 1 236 is divisible by 4, because its last two digits, 36, make a number that is divisible by 4.

4. Command terms: what the question wants

Every IB question starts with a command term. It tells you how much working to show. Match each term to what you must do.

What would you do?

1. “Show that x = 2 is a solution of x³ − 3x − 2 = 0.” What do you write?

For “show that” the answer is already given: the marks are for the steps that reach it. Substitute and show the arithmetic.

2. “Write down the value of 5⁰.” How much working do you need?

“Write down” = the answer is quick to see. One mark, no working expected.

3. (a) Expand (x + 3)². (b) Hence solve x² + 6x + 9 = 16. What does “hence” tell you?

“Hence” means you must build on the previous part. A different method, even if correct, may get no marks.

4. A question says “Find”. You write only the correct final number. What happens?

“Find” and “calculate” expect stages of working. Method marks are given for them.
