Reference

Grammar focus

Read this — not scored

Use

  • We're more interested in the action/process than the people who do it.
  • We don't know exactly who does the action.
  • It's obvious or understood who does the action.

Form

subject + be + past participle (+ by + agent)

We usually leave out "by + agent" — it often sounds unnatural and adds no new information, e.g. This invention has been used since 1908 (not "…since 1908 by people").

Examples by tense

Present simpleChildren are taught to run to the centre of the playground.
Present perfectIt has been shown that quick reactions make all the difference.
Present continuousThe volcano is being observed for further activity today.
Past simpleAn experiment was conducted.
willAll tomorrow's flights will be cancelled if the ash cloud is blown over the flight path.
canIn droughts, forest fires can be started very easily.

Grammar in context

The passive — verbs with two objects

Some verbs can be followed by two objects — usually a thing and a person. That means two passive sentences are possible:

They give residents a tsunami alert. (active)

→ A tsunami alert is given to residents. (thing as subject)

→ Residents are given a tsunami alert. (person as subject — more common)
