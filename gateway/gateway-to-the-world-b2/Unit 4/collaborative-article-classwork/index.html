The best guest! · B2 Unit 4Collaborative article · SB p.53

1. Choose the best guest

Gateway to the World B2 · Unit 4 · Developing writing · SB p.53 · 45–55 minutes

Our result: a lively school-magazine article that persuades other students to invite our chosen guest. We will introduce the person, justify our choice with examples, plan their visit and finish with a convincing recommendation.

The brief: Your school magazine wants suggestions for a famous guest. Write an article explaining who you would invite, why they would be a great guest and what you would like them to do at school.

In groups of 3–4, suggest two people. Give one reason for each, then agree on one guest. Choose somebody you already know enough about; check uncertain details before including them.

Class target: about 180–220 words for the whole article. This is a classroom target; the SB task does not specify a word limit.

Use the same room name on every device in your group. Use a new name for a new lesson.

Roles: lead writer (opening), evidence editor (reasons), visit planner (activities), closing editor (recommendation). With three students, the lead writer also writes the ending. Everyone reviews another paragraph.

2. Notice what works

Look back at the model article on SB p.53. Find one example of each technique below. Say what it makes the reader think or imagine.

Talk to the reader

you / your / we / our
Have you ever…?
Can you think of…?
Imagine… / Just think…

Make your view clear

What I like most about… is…
Personally speaking, …
Let me explain why.
What’s more, …

Turn a claim into a reason

She is inspiring. → What has she done that could inspire students? Why would it matter to us?

Discuss how to improve this flat opening: “I want to invite a famous scientist. She is very good and interesting.” Try one reader question, a specific description and a clear link to school life.

One possible upgrade (after discussing)

Have you ever wondered how scientists keep going when an experiment fails? Imagine meeting someone who could show us how a setback becomes a new idea.

This opening still needs the guest’s name and a specific, accurate example in the next paragraph.

Adjective check: very/really/extremely inspiring; absolutely/really amazing. Avoid very amazing. Choose precise descriptions rather than piling up adjectives.

3. Agree on a four-paragraph plan

Agree on the plan aloud before anyone starts writing. Keep these notes on one device or copy them into your group’s first planning paragraph in the shared space. These planning boxes are saved on this device.

Before writing: assign the four paragraphs by name. Agree on the same guest, viewpoint and tone. Add an engaging title to the first paragraph.

4. Write together

Join with your name. Each writer adds their own paragraph; everyone can see the article develop live. Add paragraphs in order A → B → C → D. If somebody’s turn comes later, prepare your wording orally first. Keep the finished article to four paragraphs.

Useful language

What I like most about … is … · … is absolutely brilliant at … · This would give us the chance to … · First, … Then, … · In the afternoon, … · Personally speaking, … · Can you think of a better …?

Wait for the green connection indicator before writing. An internet connection is required. Only your own paragraphs are editable in student mode; ask the paragraph’s author to make agreed changes.

5. Edit, read and vote

Swap reviewers: A reviews B, B reviews C, C reviews D, D reviews A. Read the whole article first, then give your partner one precise suggestion. Each author revises their own paragraph.

Useful feedback: “This reason would be more convincing if…” · “Could we show how this helps students?” · “We have already said…; perhaps add…”

Teacher notes · PDF

Return to Write together to submit your contribution with the component’s Send to teacher button. It includes the whole room. If your teacher is collecting the final group text, agree who submits it.

Read and vote

Read another group’s article using their room link. Choose the most convincing guest proposal and justify your vote with a specific reason from the article. You cannot vote for your own group.

Exit check: Which change made your article more convincing? Show the original and improved wording to the class.
