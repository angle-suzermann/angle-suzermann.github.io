Reading: how to prepare for your driving test

Passing a driving test doesn't happen overnight. Most learners need weeks — often months — of lessons with a qualified instructor before they feel ready. Booking regular lessons, even just one or two a week, usually works better than one long lesson followed by a long gap.

Alongside practical lessons, every learner must pass a theory test first, which checks your knowledge of the Highway Code and includes a hazard perception section. Many students use apps or books to practise theory questions in short daily sessions.

Practising manoeuvres — like reverse parking or a three-point turn — again and again, in different places, builds real confidence. Extra practice with a patient family member, in a car with L-plates, can help too, alongside lessons.

On the day of the test itself, small things matter: get a good night's sleep, arrive early, bring your documents, and try to breathe and drive naturally, the way you've practised. Examiners want to see safe, confident driving — not perfection.
