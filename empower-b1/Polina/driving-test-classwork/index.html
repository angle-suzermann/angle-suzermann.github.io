Learning to drive

Passing your driving test is a big moment — suddenly you can go anywhere, any time, on your own. But first there's a lot to learn: the names of all the parts of a car, the rules of the road, and how to stay calm with an examiner sitting next to you! Today's lesson will help you talk about cars and driving tests with confidence.

1. Have you (or has someone in your family) ever taken a driving test? What was it like — or what do you imagine it's like?

2. What do you think is the hardest part of learning to drive? Why?

Reading: at the driving test centre

Maya arrives at the test centre twenty minutes early — her instructor, Dan, told her never to be late. She waits with her provisional licence in her hand, watching other learner drivers walk in and out, some smiling, some not.

When the examiner calls her name, they walk out to the car together. First, he asks Maya an eyesight check: to read a number plate from twenty metres away. Then come two "show me, tell me" questions — one about how to check the oil, another about how to use the windscreen wiper. Only then does the test really begin.

For the next forty minutes, Maya drives through town and along a fast road, following the examiner's directions and using her indicator every time she turns. Halfway through, he asks her to do a manoeuvre: reverse parking between two cars. Her hands are shaking a little, but she checks her mirrors, goes slowly, and gets it right first time.

Back at the test centre, the examiner explains her result: one small mistake, but a clear pass. Maya cannot stop smiling — six months of lessons, and it was worth every minute.

Glossary: a provisional licence is a licence you get before you pass your test — it lets you drive on the road while you are still learning, as long as a qualified instructor or supervising driver is with you.

Grammar: rules for learner drivers

Look at these examples:
"You must pass a test before you drive alone." (it's necessary — a rule)
"You mustn't use your phone while driving." (it's forbidden — never do this)
"You don't have to own a car — you can borrow the school's." (it's not necessary, but it's fine if you do)
Now choose the correct word for each rule below.

1. You wear a seat belt.

2. You use a mobile phone while driving.

3. Learner drivers display L-plates on their car.

4. You take the test in your own car — you can use the driving school's.

5. You drink alcohol and then drive.

6. You be able to read a number plate from 20 metres away.
