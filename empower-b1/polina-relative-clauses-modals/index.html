Relative Clauses, Ability Modals & Mechanics

Part 1 — Relative Clauses: defining vs. non-defining

1. Fill in the correct relative pronoun (defining clause — no comma).

We visited a city we saw ancient landmarks.

2. Does this sentence need a comma before the relative clause?

We visited Beijing ___ where we saw ancient landmarks.

3. Fill in the correct relative pronoun (defining clause).

The giveaway we announced on Instagram got hundreds of entries.

4. Does this sentence need a comma before the relative clause?

The city ___ where we saw ancient landmarks was incredible.

5. Fill in the correct relative pronoun (non-defining clause — extra info about a known person).

My colleague, has lived in Beijing for ten years, gave us great tips.

Part 2 — Ability Modals: could / was able to / managed to / been able to

6. Complete with the correct ability form (general past ability).

As a teenager, she speak three languages fluently.

7. Complete with the correct ability form (succeeded at something difficult).

After trying five times, he finally open the stubborn jar.

8. Complete with the correct ability form (specific achievement despite difficulty).

Despite the rain, we finish the whole tour on foot.

9. Complete with the correct ability form (Present Perfect — "can" is impossible here).

He's think much more clearly since he started journaling.

10. Complete with the correct ability form (succeeding despite difficulty, describing a small percentage).

Only 5% of applicants pass the final round.

Part 3 — Prepositions, Articles & Mechanics
