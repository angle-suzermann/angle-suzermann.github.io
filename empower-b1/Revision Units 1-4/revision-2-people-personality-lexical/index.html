B. The phrases and their patterns

Tap a card to open it. Look at the pattern: what comes after the phrase? Then read the Watch out note: it shows the most common mistake.
get to know someoneget to know + person

start to know someone well, little by little

“Once I got to know her, I realised she's really funny.” from the text

Watch out: get to know = the process (little by little); know = the result: Now I know her well.

have a lot in commonhave a lot / nothing / so much in common (with someone)

like the same things; be similar

“We have a lot in common: we both love skating.” from the text

Watch out: not a lot of common. Opposite: have nothing in common.

take after someonetake after + older family member

be like an older person in your family (character or looks)

“Alina takes after her dad.” from the text

Watch out: take after (be similar) ≠ look after (take care of). You take after your mum, not your little sister.

be brought up by / inbe brought up + by (people) / in (a place)

be looked after and educated as a child

“She was brought up by her grandparents for a few years.” from the text

Watch out: passive: I was brought up. Or active: I grew up in…. Not I was grown up.

have a good relationship withhave a good / close / difficult relationship + with

get on well with someone

“She still has a really good relationship with her gran.” from the text

Watch out: the preposition is with, not to.

grow apartgrow apart (from someone)

slowly become less close to someone

“Some of my old school friends and I grew apart.” from the text

Watch out: past: grew apart. It happens slowly, it is not one argument.

hang out (with someone)hang out + with someone / at a place

spend free time with friends (informal)

“Alina and I hang out after practice almost every evening.” from the text

Watch out: informal: good for messages to friends, not for a formal letter.

keep in touch (with someone)keep / stay in touch + with someone

keep talking or writing to someone who lives far away

“When she goes to training camps, we always keep in touch.” from the text

Watch out: no the: keep in touch, not keep in the touch.

be determined to + verbbe determined + to + verb

want to do something very much and not stop trying

“They're both calm and very determined to win.” from the text

Watch out: determined to win, not determined for winning.

give up (+ -ing)give up + noun / -ing

stop doing something

“Last year she had a bad fall and nearly gave up skating.” from the text

Watch out: give up skating, not give up to skate.

manage to + verbmanage + to + verb

succeed in doing something difficult

“After months of hard work, she managed to land her first triple jump.” from the text

Watch out: managed to land, not managed landing.

be good at (+ -ing)be good at + noun / -ing

be able to do something well

“She's good at helping me too.” from the text

Watch out: the preposition is at: good at skating, not good in skating. (Your Unit 4D writing: able to sport → good at sport.)

Reading: a biography

Read the biography, then answer the questions.

Never too late: the story of Rita Moss

Rita Moss was born in 1950 in a small fishing town in Cornwall. Her father was a fisherman, and she used to spend hours on the beach watching the surfers, but her parents didn't allow her to go in the water. 'Girls didn't do that kind of thing,' she remembers. 'My mother was very old-fashioned.'

Rita left school at sixteen and worked in a bakery for ten years. During that time she met Tom, a quiet, reserved man who took after his father: they were both fishermen and both very shy. They got married in 1972 and had three children.

For the next forty years, Rita was too busy to think about surfing. While Tom was at sea, she looked after the children and ran the family business. Meanwhile, the town was changing: tourists were arriving every summer and surf schools were opening along the beach.

In 2020, two years after Tom had died, Rita's granddaughter Mia persuaded her to try a lesson. 'I was terrified,' Rita laughs. 'I couldn't swim very well, and the water was absolutely freezing!' She fell off the board forty times that day. But she didn't give up. After three months of lessons, she managed to stand up on a wave for the first time.

Today, at 76, Rita surfs three times a week until the weather gets too cold. She has become quite famous: a video of her surfing has had over two million views. 'People ask me if I'm brave,' she says. 'I'm not. I'm just determined. It's never too late to do the thing you always wanted to do.'
