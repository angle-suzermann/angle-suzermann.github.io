Revision 4: Behind the Wheel

This homework uses learning to drive to help you revise and recycle the language from Units 1–4 of Empower B1+. You'll read, listen, write and talk about lessons, tests and journeys that went wrong. It takes about 60–75 minutes, so you can do it in two or three sessions.

What you'll practise

  • Vocabulary: driving phrases: find them in a text, choose the right verb (take, make, do, get), use them
  • Grammar: present perfect, past simple and present perfect continuous, present tenses, narrative tenses, used to, ability, articles
  • Reading: an email from a friend who has just passed her test
  • Listening: a phone call after a driving test
  • Everyday English: telling a story, asking for help, suggestions, question tags
  • Writing: an email about a journey or lesson that went wrong
  • Your feedback: prepositions and word choice (from your Unit 4D writing)
  • Speaking: talk about driving and record it for your teacher

“I started to take driving lessons in September.” from the text

Watch out: take or have lessons, not make or do lessons.

make a mistakemake + a mistake / mistakes

do something wrong

“In my first lesson I made a mistake every two minutes.” from the text

Watch out: we say make a mistake, not do a mistake.

check the mirrorscheck + the mirrors / the petrol / the tyres

look in the mirrors to see what is behind you

“I forgot to check the mirrors.” from the text

Watch out: check the mirrors or check your mirrors.

keep to the speed limitkeep to + the speed limit

not drive faster than you are allowed to

“I couldn't keep to the speed limit: I was either too slow or too fast.” from the text

Watch out: opposite: break the speed limit (drive too fast).

do a parallel parkdo + a parallel park / a three-point turn

park between two cars at the side of the road

“I still can't do a parallel park.” from the text

Watch out: the verb is do; you can also just say park: I parked perfectly.

go round a roundaboutgo round + a roundabout · take the second exit

drive round a circle where several roads meet

“The worst moment was when I had to go round a roundabout for the first time.” from the text

Watch out: at a roundabout (place) · go round a roundabout (movement).

stop at a red lightstop at + a red light / the traffic lights

stop the car when the light is red

“I was so nervous that I stopped at a green light!” from the text

Watch out: the preposition is at: stop at the lights, not on.

get lostget lost (past: got lost)

not know where you are and how to get to the right place

“We drove to a village I didn't know, and of course we got lost.” from the text

Watch out: We got lost. We lost means that you didn't win a game.

run out of petrolrun out of + petrol / time / money

have no more petrol left

“Then we nearly ran out of petrol.” from the text

Watch out: past: ran out of; don't forget of.

book a testbook + a test / a lesson / a ticket

arrange the day and time of your test

“I've booked my test for December.” from the text

Watch out: book a test, not order a test.

pass a testtake a test (= do it) · pass a test (= succeed) · fail a test

succeed in a test

“I really want to pass it first time.” from the text

Watch out: take a test = do it (Russian сдавать); pass = do it successfully (сдать). I'm taking my test tomorrow, not I'm passing my test tomorrow.

give someone a liftgive + person + a lift (to + place)

take someone somewhere in your car

“If I do, I'll give my mum a lift to work every morning.” from the text

Watch out: word order: give my mum a lift; ask for one: Could you give me a lift?

Operation · judge: possible or not?

E. Possible or not natural?

Is the phrase used correctly? Choose, then read why.

1. I'm passing my driving test tomorrow, wish me luck!

To do a test = take a test: I'm taking my test tomorrow. Pass = succeed.

2. I did a big mistake at the roundabout.

We say make a mistake: I made a big mistake.

3. Could you give me a lift to the station?

Correct: give me a lift to a place.

4. We ran out of petrol on the way home.

Correct: run out of something.

5. I made driving lessons last summer.

We take or have lessons: I took driving lessons.

6. Stop on the red light.

The preposition is at: stop at the red light.

7. We lost on the way to the lake.

We got lost = we didn't know where we were. We lost = we didn't win.

8. The test centre is very convenient: it's near my house.

Correct: convenient = easy, saves time (your feedback page: not comfortable).

B. Your email

Task
Write an email (120–150 words) to a friend about a driving lesson, a journey or a trip that went wrong. It can be true or invented!
  • Start with a hook: You won't believe what happened…
  • Tell the story with narrative tenses (We were driving… when… We had forgotten…).
  • Use story phrases: To make matters worse… It turned out that… In the end…
  • Add information with besides, apart from, in addition.
  • Finish in a friendly way.
Phrases to use
