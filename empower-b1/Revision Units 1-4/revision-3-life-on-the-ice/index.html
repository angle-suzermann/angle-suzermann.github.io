Reading: a day on the ice

Read the article, then answer the questions.

Behind the sparkle

When you watch figure skating on TV, it looks easy: beautiful costumes, music and smiles. But what does it really take? We spent a day with Alina Petrova, 17, who has been skating since she was five.

5.30 a.m. Alina's alarm goes off. 'Most of my friends are still asleep,' she says. 'I used to hate early mornings, but now I don't even think about it.' She has a quick breakfast (porridge and a banana) and her dad drives her to the rink.

7.00 a.m. The first session starts with a thirty-minute warm-up off the ice: running, stretching and jumping practice. 'People think we just skate, but we're athletes,' Alina explains. 'Our legs have to be incredibly strong.'

7.30 a.m. On the ice, her coach, Irina, watches every move. She's strict, and she doesn't give compliments easily. 'When she says "not bad", I know I've done something brilliant,' Alina laughs. Today they're working on a triple Salchow. Alina falls six times. On the seventh try, she lands it.

1.00 p.m. School. Alina studies online in the afternoons, so she can fit her lessons around training. 'It isn't easy. Sometimes I'm so tired that I can't concentrate.'

5.00 p.m. The second session: choreography. This is Alina's favourite part. 'Jumps get you points, but the programme is where you tell a story. I want the judges to feel something.'

9.30 p.m. Bed. Alina's advice for young skaters? 'Enjoy it. If you're only thinking about medals, you'll give up. The best skaters are the ones who love being on the ice, even when it's absolutely freezing.'

Task

  • Start with a short introduction (why competitions can be scary).
  • Give 3–4 tips. Explain the purpose with to, in order to, so that.

  • Finish with some encouragement.

Host: Welcome back to Rink Talk. My guest today is Kate Morgan, who has just won the silver medal at the national championships. Kate, congratulations!

Kate: Thank you! I still can't believe it.

Host: How long have you been skating?

Kate: Since I was five. My mum used to take me to the rink every Saturday morning, and I loved it straight away, even though it was absolutely freezing!

Host: And how often do you train now?

Kate: Six days a week. I usually get up at half past five, and I'm on the ice by seven. This month I've been working on a new jump, the triple loop.

Host: Have you landed it yet?

Kate: Not in competition. I've managed to land it a few times in training, but I haven't been able to do it under pressure yet.

Host: Tell us about the championships. What happened in your free programme?

Kate: Well, it started really badly. I was doing my first jump when my blade caught a mark on the ice, and I fell. I'd practised that jump hundreds of times, so I was furious with myself.

Host: But you didn't give up.

Kate: No. My coach always says the next element is the most important one. So I got up, I listened to the music, and I managed to finish the rest of the programme without any mistakes.

Host: What makes a good skater, in your opinion?

Kate: If you ask me, talent isn't enough. You need to be determined, and you need a positive attitude towards mistakes, because you're going to fall, a lot! Some skaters are very sensitive to criticism, and that's hard in this sport.

Host: And what about your coach? What's she like?

Kate: She's quite serious and reserved, but she's brilliant. She can see a tiny problem in a jump that nobody else notices.

Host: Finally, what's next for you?

Kate: I'm hoping to be able to compete internationally next season. And I'd love to do the triple loop in front of the judges!

Host: Kate Morgan, thank you, and good luck!
