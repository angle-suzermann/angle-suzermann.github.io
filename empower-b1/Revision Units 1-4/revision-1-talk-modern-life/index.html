Five ways to make your language app actually work

by Sofia Reyes · 12 comments

I've been using language apps for three years now, and I've tried almost all of them. Some were brilliant; others were absolutely useless. Most people download an app, use it every day for a week, and then forget about it. Sound familiar? Here are five tips to help you keep going.

1 Set a tiny goal. Five minutes a day is enough. It doesn't sound like much, but it adds up to more than thirty hours a year. Do your five minutes at the same time every day so that it becomes a habit, like brushing your teeth.

2 Turn off the sound effects. Those happy little noises feel great at first, but after a while they become really annoying. Switch them off in order to concentrate on the language, not the game.

3 Say everything out loud. Apps are good at teaching you to recognise words, but they can't make you speak. Repeat every sentence you hear. This will help you with pronunciation, and it will make you more confident when you finally talk to people face-to-face.

4 Don't worry about the streak. Many apps count how many days in a row you've studied. A friend of mine had a 400-day streak and was absolutely furious when she lost it on holiday. She almost gave up completely! Remember: the number isn't important – the learning is.

5 Find real people. An app is a great start, but language is about communication. Join an online group or find a conversation partner to practise with. They'll teach you things no app can: jokes, slang and how people really talk.

Task
Write an email (120–150 words) to a friend you haven't talked to for a while. Tell them your news: something new you've started (a job, a course, a sport, an app…).
  • Say sorry for not writing sooner.
  • Give your news. Use the present perfect and present perfect continuous (I've started… I've been learning…).
  • Add information with besides, apart from, in addition.
  • Suggest meeting up (Why don't we…? How about…?).
  • Finish in a friendly way (See you soon, Love, Take care).
