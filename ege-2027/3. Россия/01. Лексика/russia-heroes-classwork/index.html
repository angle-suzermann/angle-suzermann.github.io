Noticing · Heroes of Russia

Прочитай и заметь блоки

Прочитай тексты целиком: жёлтым выделены чанки. Потом найди в текстах блок для каждой русской фразы.

Richard Sorge · intelligence officer

Richard Sorge (1895–1944) was a Soviet intelligence officer who worked undercover in Japan before and during World War II. His reports gave the Soviet leadership critical information, including warnings about Germany’s planned invasion and Japan’s decision not to attack the USSR in 1941. His work required extraordinary courage and the willingness to put the common good above personal safety. For decades his name was unknown to the general public, yet his contribution to the victory was immense.

Fyodor Ushakov · admiral

Admiral Fyodor Ushakov (1745–1817) was a legendary Russian naval commander who never lost a single battle. He was known for his concern for the lives of his sailors and tried to end battles quickly to save human lives. He combined military courage with deep faith and modesty, and in 2001 the Russian Orthodox Church declared him a saint.

Alexey Maresyev · fighter pilot

Alexey Maresyev (1916–2001) was a Soviet fighter pilot. In 1942 his plane was shot down, and he survived in the winter forest for 18 days with badly injured legs, which were later amputated. Despite this, he taught himself to fly with artificial legs and returned to combat. His story became the basis of Boris Polevoy’s novel The Story of a Real Man. It shows that human dignity does not depend on physical abilities and that true courage means overcoming a terrible ordeal.

Dmitry Donskoy · prince and commander

Prince Dmitry Donskoy (1350–1389) led the Russian forces to victory at the Battle of Kulikovo in 1380. The battle took place near the Don River, in today’s Tula Region, and became a turning point in the struggle against the Mongol-Tatar yoke. The prince managed to unite the Russian lands around a common goal.

Yerofey Khabarov · explorer

Yerofey Khabarov (c. 1603–1671) was a Russian explorer who led expeditions along the Amur River in the mid-17th century. His journeys helped to map Russia’s Far Eastern lands. An explorer had to act in conditions of uncertainty, take responsibility for his companions and survive harsh natural conditions. The city of Khabarovsk is named after him.
