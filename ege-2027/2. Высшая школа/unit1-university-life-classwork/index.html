5 Reading: Harvard University

Read the text. Some words are highlighted — you'll need them for Task 5 below.

Harvard University is a private university in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and a member of the Ivy League, with about 6,700 undergraduate students and about 13,100 postgraduate students.

The term Ivy League is used to refer to eight schools as a group of elite colleges beyond the sports context. The eight members are Brown University, Columbia University, Cornell University, Dartmouth College, Harvard University, the University of Pennsylvania, Princeton University and Yale University.

Harvard was started on September 8, 1636, which makes it the oldest institution of higher learning in the United States. It was named after its first benefactor, the clergyman John Harvard. Eight Presidents of the United States have graduated from Harvard college and its schools.

Harvard is on the Charles River. Some students go there for rowing; on the other side of the river is the city of Boston. Most of Harvard's schools are in Cambridge, though some of its graduate schools are in Boston — together, they make up Harvard University. The school colour is crimson, a dark red.

Harvard is devoted to excellence in teaching, learning and research, and to developing leaders who make a difference globally. The university has an enrolment of over 20,000 degree candidates, including undergraduate, graduate and professional students. Harvard has more than 360,000 alumni (singular: alumnus) around the world.

Did you know? In the USA, "school" can mean almost any place of learning — even a university. A two-year college gives an associate's degree; a four-year college or university gives a bachelor's degree (this level is called undergraduate). A university also has graduate (postgraduate) schools, offering a master's or a doctorate — so a university covers both levels, while "college" in everyday US speech usually means the undergraduate level only.

8 Reading: University Life in the UK

Five sentences have been removed from the text. In the style of the ЕГЭ reading section, match each gap (1–5) with the sentence (A–E) that fits it. Click a sentence in the box, then click the gap.

There are over 1.5 million full-time university students in the UK, studying at over 100 universities across the country.

Students usually require three or four good A-level grades to get a place at university. For most courses, students apply in the January of their final year of school.

Most degree courses are three years long, but four-year courses are becoming more popular, with one year spent either abroad or working in industry.

Most British campus universities were built in the 1960s and 70s. Traditionally, universities had grown in the centres of cities.

At campus universities, students' social lives centre mainly around the student union. Wednesday afternoons are usually reserved for sport at UK universities, with inter-university league matches happening across the country in a wide range of sports.

Large lecture theatres can hold up to 300 students. Students may also attend smaller lectures and seminars, where more student participation is encouraged.

Students rely more and more on online learning as part of their university studies these days.

Most UK students attend universities quite far away from their home towns. At campus universities, most first-year students live on-site in halls of residence. The accommodation is owned by the university, and students usually have their own room, with internet access.

In the UK, university is often seen as a stepping-stone to adulthood, and is considered to be as much about life experience as it is about getting a degree.
