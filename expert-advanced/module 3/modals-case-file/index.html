Stage 1 / 7

Mission Briefing

Welcome to the Modal Verbs Division. Before you're sent out into the field, you need to prove you can handle every kind of modal verb an investigator meets on the job — from ordering a rookie about, to working out who's lying, to politely asking a suspect a question.

This case file is not a repeat of your coursebook exercises — every sentence here is new. It reviews exactly the same ground your Student's Book covered on pages 50–51, with the reference grammar on pages 178–179, but you'll meet it in fresh contexts.

By the end of this case file, you will be able to:
  • choose the right modal (or semi-modal) for obligation, prohibition, advice, permission, ability, possibility, deduction, and offers/requests
  • tell the difference between close pairs examiners love to test: mustn't vs. can't, needn't have vs. didn't need to, could vs. was able to
  • spot and correct the most common modal mistakes at this level
  • use past modals (must have done, needn't have done, should have done) accurately in connected speech
  • hold a natural, unscripted conversation using a wide range of modals under time pressure

Stage 7 / 7 — Freer Practice

The Interrogation Room

Work with a partner (or record yourself doing both sides). One of you is the Detective, the other is the Witness. Improvise a 3–4 minute conversation about a (invented) incident you both agree on in 10 seconds before you start — e.g. a break-in, a missing pet, a workplace mix-up. Try to naturally use modals from every category in the Field Manual.

🕵️ Detective

  • Ask permission questions (Might I…? / Would you mind if…?)
  • State obligations (You'll have to… / You need to…)
  • Make deductions out loud (You must have… / You can't have…)
  • Give advice or a warning (You'd better… / You should…)

🙋 Witness

  • Talk about what you can/could and couldn't do that night
  • Speculate about what might have happened (It could have been…)
  • Criticise your own past actions (I shouldn't have… / I needn't have…)
  • Offer to help further (I could… / Shall I…?)
