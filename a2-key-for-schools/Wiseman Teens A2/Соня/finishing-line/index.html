Wiseman Teens A2 · The finishing line

Two lessons before the exam on 29 November. Today we repair the points you lost in Homework 4 — so that on Sunday they don’t cost you anything.

After this lesson you can:
  • answer all three points of an email — one sentence for each point, with the words from the task;
  • in Listening, catch the trap: you often hear all three answers, but only one answers the question;
  • fill small grammar gaps: much, for, would, of, which, what;
  • use the past correctly: I played, I felt, I left it at home.

Listening · Catch the trap

These are the questions you lost in Homework 4. Listen to the part again, choose — then read why.

Rule: you usually hear all three answers. Listen for but, not, like today, next door — the words that say no to the trap. And never leave an answer blank: a guess can be right, an empty box can’t.

Task 4 · Hitomi and Freddie

Task 5 · five short texts

Task 3 · Angela and her uncle

1 L4 · Who did Hitomi go to Hardin Castle with?

“My class visited a factory. I went with the family who live next door to us.” Family is a trap — next door = neighbours.

2 L4 · What was the weather like?

“It rained… It wasn’t cold and windy like today.” Windy is about today, not Saturday. Rained = wet.

3 L5 · Why are they going to the city centre?

“we’ll be at the cafe… in 30 minutes” — to meet Mum. The football match and the newspaper are traps.

4 L5 · What information is the teacher giving?

“we’re allowed to go to the park today” = outside. The project is old (“you still need some photos”).

5 L5 · Who has Emma married?

“Her husband… showing tourists round different cities.” Artist is Emma herself; the restaurant is only where she lives.

6 L3 · The cafeteria was…

“you could always hear the traffic… or the crowds” — hear = noisy. The crowds were outside.

8:00

Will’s blog

One day my dad said, ‘Why don’t we have a street party?’ This means that the street is closed so cars can’t use it, and people put tables and chairs out in the street, then have a party! Dad said there was one in 1977 and he still remembers it well. Everyone loved it! I couldn’t believe that since 1977 they never had another one. If it was so good, why not do it again?

We started to organise it, together with some other people. I helped to make the web page, so everyone on the street knew about the party and could post their old photos from the party in 1977. There were some pictures of my dad when he was a kid, together with his friends, who have moved away from the street now. It was interesting to see that the buildings on the street haven’t changed at all!

My mum was a bit worried about the party. ‘But a lot of people on the street don’t really know each other’, she said. ‘What if they don’t have anything to talk about?’ I just said, ‘Relax, Mum. It’ll be great.’

So, what was the party like? It was fantastic! My friends and I really liked speaking to an old lady called Louisa. She’s 89 and was telling us about when she and her friends were our age. So now I always chat to her when I see her on the street. I didn’t know who she was before, so I’m glad we had the party.

1 Why was Will surprised?

“I couldn’t believe that since 1977 they never had another one.” couldn’t believe = surprised.

2 What did the photos from 1977 show?

“the buildings on the street haven’t changed at all!” C — his dad’s friends have moved away.

3 Why was Will’s mother worried?

“What if they don’t have anything to talk about?”

4 Will and his friends enjoyed

“She… was telling us about when she and her friends were our age.” She told the stories; they didn’t meet her friends.

5 What is the best title for the article?

Check every paragraph: the idea, the photos, Mum, the party — all about one party. A — only one party; C — he doesn’t give instructions.
