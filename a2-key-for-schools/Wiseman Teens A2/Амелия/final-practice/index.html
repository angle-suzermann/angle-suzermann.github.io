Wiseman Teens A2 · Final practice

This is our last lesson before the exam on 29 November. We do the Speaking part like in the exam, one reading task, an email and a story on time, and the rules for the exam day.

After this lesson you can:
  • talk about every picture in 2–3 sentences, not one word — now, yesterday, tomorrow and a chart;
  • give a full answer to a chart question: No, it isn’t. Sports are more popular than shopping.
  • write a story where the events follow from each other, in the past, in 45–60 words;
  • prove a reading answer with a sentence from the text.
Exam strategy: never stay silent. If you don’t understand, say “Could you repeat the question, please?”

Warm-up · Like the start of the exam

Answer in full sentences. After each answer, add one more detail.

  • What’s your name? How old are you?
  • Where do you go to school? What’s your favourite subject? Why?
  • What did you do last weekend?
  • What do you usually do after school?
  • What are you going to do after the exam?
One more detail: …because… / …and then… / …with my…

Speaking · Picture — Today

Look at the pictures. Tell me what the people are doing now. Talk about each picture — about 1 minute.

Questions
  • What is happening in the first picture?
  • What are the people in the park doing?
  • What is the boy in the last picture doing?
  • Where would you like to eat today? Why?

Not one word for all the pictures — one or two sentences for each: In the first picture, a boy is buying a burger. In the second picture, a family is having a picnic…

Speaking · Picture — Yesterday

Imagine you ate at one of these places yesterday. Tell me about it.

Questions
  • Where did you eat? Who did you go with?
  • What did you eat and drink?
  • What happened? Was it good? Why?

Past Simple: we went, I ate, we had, it was · background + event: We were eating when it started to rain.

Will’s blog

One day my dad said, ‘Why don’t we have a street party?’ This means that the street is closed so cars can’t use it, and people put tables and chairs out in the street, then have a party! Dad said there was one in 1977 and he still remembers it well. Everyone loved it! I couldn’t believe that since 1977 they never had another one. If it was so good, why not do it again?

We started to organise it, together with some other people. I helped to make the web page, so everyone on the street knew about the party and could post their old photos from the party in 1977. There were some pictures of my dad when he was a kid, together with his friends, who have moved away from the street now. It was interesting to see that the buildings on the street haven’t changed at all!

My mum was a bit worried about the party. ‘But a lot of people on the street don’t really know each other’, she said. ‘What if they don’t have anything to talk about?’ I just said, ‘Relax, Mum. It’ll be great.’

So, what was the party like? It was fantastic! My friends and I really liked speaking to an old lady called Louisa. She’s 89 and was telling us about when she and her friends were our age. So now I always chat to her when I see her on the street. I didn’t know who she was before, so I’m glad we had the party.

1 Why was Will surprised?

Proof: “I couldn’t believe that since 1977 they never had another one.” couldn’t believe = surprised.

2 What did the photos from 1977 show?

Proof: “the buildings on the street haven’t changed at all!” C — his dad’s friends have moved away.

3 Why was Will’s mother worried?

Proof: “What if they don’t have anything to talk about?”

4 Will and his friends enjoyed

Proof: “She… was telling us about when she and her friends were our age.” Friends is in the text — but they didn’t meet them; and she told the stories, not they.

5 What is the best title for the article?

Proof: every paragraph is about one party — the idea, the photos, Mum, the party itself.

Writing · Task 7 · 1 + 10 minutes

Write a story in 45–60 words. Start with this sentence:

We were having dinner at home when suddenly all the lights went out.

Plan in 1 minute (say it aloud first):

  • What did you do first — and why?
  • What happened next?
  • How did it end? How did you feel?

0 words
Check in 2 minutes
  • You do something in the story — not only other people.
  • Every event follows from the one before; the ending is possible in real life.
  • Past Simple for what happened: found, lit, called, laughed · there was, not there’s.
  • another friend · the other people · clothes = them.
  • 45–60 words, with the first sentence.
