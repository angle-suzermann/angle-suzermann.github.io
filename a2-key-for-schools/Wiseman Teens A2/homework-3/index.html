Language · Usually, yesterday or now?

Complete the gaps with the correct form of the verb in brackets. Look for time clues.

1 My sister her homework after lunch every day. (do)

2 Yesterday I the last question. (not understand)

3 Look! Our teacher . (come)

4 My brother interested in cooking. He prefers football. (not be)

5 We a video in the park yesterday. (make)

6 My fingers red after our walk yesterday. (be)

7 I can’t talk now. I for my keys. (look)

8 He the answer yesterday, so he asked the teacher. (not know)

After your first try: a reminder

Every day: he does. Yesterday: he didn’t understand, not didn’t understood. Now: he is coming. A state: he isn’t interested.

Language · Say what you mean

Choose the phrase that fits the meaning.

1 It is cold outside. I feel well, but I need a warm coat.

Cold weather or feeling cold is different from having a cold.

2 I am ill: my nose is running and I am sneezing.

An illness: have a cold.

3 This is ___ tablet. The other one is yours.

Use my before a noun.

4 That tablet is ___.

Mine replaces my tablet.

5 Anna is good ___ explaining new games.

The phrase is good at + -ing.

6 The lessons do not cost too ___ money.

Money is uncountable: too much money.

You cannot find your tablet. You think it is under some clothes on a chair. Write one simple sentence to tell a friend where to look.

Reading · Change your mind

Read the text. Choose your answers, then give two short pieces of evidence.

A workshop worth trying

I usually spend Saturday mornings playing computer games, so I wasn’t keen to go to a cooking workshop with my cousin. I thought it would be boring. But when we arrived, the teacher let us choose what to make. I chose vegetable soup.

The teacher showed us each step with pictures. I expected it to be difficult, but I made the soup without any help. It tasted great and the teacher said it was good for us, too. We didn’t use any expensive ingredients.

The kitchen looked tiny in the photos online. In fact, there was room for twenty people! At the end, my cousin wanted to leave, but I asked about the next workshop. I want to make bread next time. If you think cooking classes are dull, try this one!

1 How did the writer feel before the workshop?

“I wasn’t keen to go” and “I thought it would be boring.”

2 What surprised the writer about making the soup?

“I expected it to be difficult, but I made the soup without any help.”

3 The soup was healthy. Which phrase shows this?

Good for us means healthy; tasty alone does not prove healthy.

4 What surprised the writer about the kitchen?

Tiny in the photos, but room for twenty people.

5 Why did the writer write this text?

The final sentence invites other people to try it; no changes or full recipe are given.

Choose TWO questions. Write the question number and copy a short phrase from the text that proves your answer.

Writing · Make the connection

Choose the link that makes the events logical.

1 We wanted to play outside, ___ it started raining.

The rain contrasts with the plan.

2 The park was closed, ___ we went to the sports centre instead.

Going elsewhere is the result of the park being closed.

3 I called my friend ___ I needed help.

Needing help is the reason for calling.

4 We found the keys under a chair. ___, we could go home.

This gives the final result of the story.

Rewrite as ONE or TWO connected sentences: “We had a great day. We decided to meet again next Saturday.” Make the connection clear.

Writing · Ask for useful help

You were in town with your English friend Sam yesterday. Now you cannot find your headphones. Write an email to Sam in 35–50 words.

  • Say what you have lost.
  • Explain where you think you left it.
  • Ask Sam to do something specific to help.
You can write “I think I left…” to explain how you may have lost something. Keep the place connected to your trip into town.

Write your email. Before you send it, check that Sam knows exactly how to help.

Writing · What happened next?

Write a story in 45–60 words. Begin with this sentence:

I was ready to leave when I noticed something strange.

Continue with your own ideas. What did you notice? What did you do? How did it end? Include the opening sentence in your word count.

Write a connected story. Use past forms. Choose the linking words you need; you do not have to use all of them.

Check: the events follow from the beginning; the reader understands why things happen; the story has an ending. No pictures — your ideas are your own.

Speaking · Tell me about your day

Practise speaking for 45–60 seconds. Use key words, not a full written script.

  • What did you do yesterday?
  • How did you feel? Why?
  • What do you usually do on a free day?
  • What are you looking forward to?

Write 4–6 KEY WORDS. Then look away from the screen and say your answer aloud twice.
